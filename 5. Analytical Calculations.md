## Part 1 :- Inverted pendulum model

The wheels roll along the ground - horizontal motion (x)

The body can lean backward - rotational motion angle $$(\theta)$$

These two motions are coupled - if the wheels accelerate the body wants to fall backward (for example bus stationary and later in moving that time human body tries to matches the bus motion)

Thats called inverted inverted pendulum on a segway means a pendulum balanced upside down , pivoting on a segway that can move

## Part 4 :- Kinetic energy 

### A) The segway moving horizontally 

Tcart = $$\frac{1}{2}mx^2$$

m = 12kg of motor + wheels 

### B) The wheels spinning 

Twheel = $$\frac{1}{2}Iwheel(\theta^2)$$

Where $$\theta$$ is the wheel spin angle. Since the wheels rolls without slipping $$\dot{\theta}$$ = $$\dot{x}/x$$ (spin rate is directly to linear speed divided by radius) this is the rolling without slipping 

### C) The body :-
If the segway moves right , body pivot point moves right too. If the body moves up/down in y then,

Xbody = $$x + Lsin\theta$$ (segway position + horizontal offset from leaning)

Y body = $$Lcos\theta$$ (height or CG)

L = 0.883m is the arm length 

$$\dot{x} body = \dot{x} + L\dot{\theta}cos\theta, \dot{y}body = -L\dot{\theta}sin\theta$$

Now the bodys total KE = translational KE of its CG + rotational KE about its own CG

Tbody = $$\frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x^2}body + \dot{y^2}body) + \frac{1}{2}I\dot{\theta^2}$$

Expanding \dot{x^2}body + \dot{y^2}body by using $$sin^2\theta + cos^2\theta$$ = 1

Therefore Tbody = $$\frac{1}{2}m \dot{x^2} + mL\dot{x}\dot{\theta}cos\theta + \frac{1}{2}mL^2\dot{\theta^2}+ \frac{1}{2}I\dot{\theta^2}$$

The term mL^2\dot{\theta^2} is coupling term.Its the mathematical proof that segway and body tilt affects eachother. Tis term is the reason a segway cant be controlled as two seperate simple systems.

## Part 5 :- Potential Energy 

Only the bodys height changes 

V = $$mgLcos\theta$$

## Part 6 :- Eular lagrange equation turning energy into motion equation

L = T - V (Kinetic - Potentail)

Therefore $$\frac{\partial}{\partial t} (\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q} = Q$$

This means take the derivative of L with respect to velocity then differential that with respect to time , the subtract the derivative of L with position, the result equals any external force or torque applied;

$$(\frac{M + Iwheel}{r^2} + m)\ddot{x} + ml\ddot{\theta}cos\theta - mL\dot{\theta^2}sin\theta = F$$

$$ml\ddot{x}cos\theta + (I + mL^2)\ddot{\theta} - mgLsin\theta = 0$$

## Part 8 :- Solving 

M = 12

m = 153 (165 - 12 = 153)

L = 0.883m

### A] Carmers Rule 
a1u + b1v = c1 

a2u + b2v = c2

where u,v = $$\ddot{x},\ddot{\theta}$$

### 1. Computing the main determinant D from coefficient of u and v

D = a1b2 - a2b1

### 2. Solve for u 

$$u = \frac{a1b2 - a2b1}{D}$$

### 3. Solve for v

$$v = \frac{a1b2 - a2b1}{D}$$

#### Applying it in equations 

$$171\ddot{x} + 135.1\ddot{\theta} = F (a1 = 171 , b1 = 135.1 , c1 = F)$$

$$135.1\ddot{x} + 129.23\ddot{\theta} = 1325.1\theta (a2 = 135.1 , b2 = 129.23 , c2 = 1325.1\theta)$$

#### Step 1 :- Compute D

D = a1b2 - a2b1 = (171)(129.23) - (135.1)(135.1)

D = 22098.3 - 18252 = 3846.3

#### Step 2 :- Solving $$\ddot{x}$$

$$\ddot{x} = \frac{a1b2 - a2b1}{D} = \frac{F(129.23) - (1325.1\theta)(135.1)}{3846.3}$$

$$\ddot{x} = \frac{129.23F - 179020\theta}{3846.3}$$

$$\ddot{x} = 0.0336F - 46.55\theta$$ 

#### Step 3 :- SOlving $$\ddot{\theta}

$$\ddot{\theta} = \frac{a1b2 - a2b1}{D}$$

$$\ddot{\theta} = \frac{(171)(1325.1\theta) - (135.1)(F)}{3846.3}$$

$$\ddot{\theta} = \frac{226592\theta - 135.1F}{3846.3}$$

Both Divide

$$\ddot{\theta} = \frac{226592\theta}{3846.3} - \frac{135.1F}{3846.3} = 58.92\theta - 0.0351F$$
